In this post, we argue that The Big Lebowski is the conflict between the German tendency to go all in and the Dude's trusting nature.

Let's look at the inputs and the outputs of the movie in terms of life, i.e., leaving out the property damage of the car, rug, etc.

*Input*: Donny dying of a heart attack, the German woman's toe, Walter beating the Germans, and The Dude getting beaten by a few characters.

*Output*: The Dude's offspring by Maude.

This is approximately all the direct effects on life in the movie. The rest is essentially a philosophical conflict between the naivety of Bunny's German friends and The Dude.

Once the Germans believe that The Dude is the one with the money, in spite of visiting his house, which is clearly decrepit, they fail to believe that he is not a millionaire. Once they make up their mind that they need to extort the Dude, they seem to naively go all in, even when presented with lots and lots of contradictory empirical evidence. They threaten him with harm multiple times and make visits to his house, burn his car, and never reflect that their fundamental assumptions may be wrong.

The Dude seems to be a firm believer of the "What's mine is mine" ethos, and totally trusts what the Germans and Lebowski say. In spite of being basically told by Walter exactly what was happening, he seems to have gotten stuck on reinstating his rug, and naively believes in everything people around him say. And, in the end, he is unable to get his rug.

The natural justice of the film is that for all his travails the Dude comes across a person with a complementary personality in the form of Maude, who's very serious about having a child with him.

Thus, in this post, we observe that The Big Lebowski can be considered a conflict between the German tendency to go all in and the Dude's trusting and non-judgemental personality.
