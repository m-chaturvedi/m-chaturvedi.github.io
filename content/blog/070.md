Chiragh Hasan Hasrat sung beautifully by Farida Khanum.  An attempt at a
literal translation.

<i>Yaa rab gham-e-*hijran mein itna to kiya hota, <br>
Jo haath jigar par hai, wo dast-e-duaa hota</i>

Oh God, you could've at least made me suffer the pain of separation with grace,<br>
The hand I use to comfort my liver should have been used for praying<br>
[i.e.
instead of complaining and crying in pain, I should be gracefully praying]

<i>Ummeed to bandh jaati, *taskeen to ho jaati,<br>
Waada na wafa karte, waada to kiya hota</i>

It would have given me some relief and hope,<br>
Even if you had made a false promise

[<br>
And we're reminded of Ghalib's famous:<br>
<i>  Tere vaade pe jiye hum, to ye jaan jhooth jaana,<br>
  Ke khushi se mar na jaate, gar aitbaar hota<br></i>
  [Translation, here](http://www.columbia.edu/itc/mealac/pritchett/00ghalib/020/20_02.html)<br>

<i>  Ta phir na intezaar mein neend aaye umra bhar,<br>
  Aane ka ahad kar gaye, aaye jo khwaab mein<br></i>
  [Translation, here](http://www.columbia.edu/itc/mealac/pritchett/00ghalib/097/97_03.html)<br>
]

<i>Gairon se kaha tumne, gairon se suna tumne,<br>
Kuch hum se kaha hota, kuch humse suna hota<br></i>

You discuss your problems with strangers, and expect sympathy<br>
You should have spoken to me about them

<i>Ek ishq ka gham aafat, aur uspe ye dil aafat,<br>
Ya gham na diya hota, ya dil na diya hota<br></i>

[This is written with an undertone of exasperation caused due to the travails of love]<br>
The pain of love in itself is a big nuisance and added to the annoyances of the desirous heart,<br>
God should have either not given me the pain or not given me the heart

---- Chiragh Hasan Hasrat
