<i>Ye arzoo thi tujhe gul ke rubaroo karte<br>
Hum aur bulbul-e-betaab guftgoo karte</i>

<i>I wished that I could make you face a flower<br>
And then try to talk to the flustered nightingale</i><br>
[In Urdu poetry, Nightangle and Flower are considered lovers. The writer is saying that She's so beautiful that the nightingale will get confused between Her and the flower and that he'll then enjoy talking to the nightingale.]

<i>*Payaambar na *mayassar hua to khoob hua,<br>
Zaabaan-e-*gair se kya *sharh-e-arzoo karte</i>

It's good if a messenger couldn't be afforded<br>
How would I have conveyed my heart through a stranger

<i>Humesha maine *girebaan ko chaak-chaak kiya,<br>
Tamaam umra *rafookar rahe rafoo karte</i>

I always got my collar torn (metaphor for public disgrace)<br>
And the cloth-menders spent their lives mending it<br>
[Meaning that I got myself in trouble constantly and my friends always tried to salvage the situation]

<i>Meri tarah se *mah-o-*mehr bhi hein aawaara,<br>
Kisi *habeeb ki ye bhi hein justjoo karte</i>

The sun and the moon also loiterers like me.<br>
They are also, like me, trying to find a lover<br>
[The relative rotation of the sun and moon around the earth is compared to loitering]

<i>Jo dekhte teri zanjeer-e-zulf ka aalam<br>
*Aseer hone ki aazaad aarzoo karte</i>

If only they witness the charm of being 'fettered' by Your tresses<br>
The Free would wish to be captured

<i>Na pooch *aalam-e-barghashta-taala'i 'Aatish'<br>
Barasti aag jo *baaraan ki arzoo karte</i>

Don't even ask about the ill fortune of "Aatish"<br>
It rained fire when he wished for rain.<br>
[I think there's wordplay in this couplet. Aatish, the writer's nom de plume means fire (a common metaphor for desires), so he's wishing for rain, meaning fulfilling his desires? But instead, the rain Gods rain fire, giving him more desires. We get reminded of Momin's:<br>
<i>Maanga karenge ab se dua hijr-e-yaar ki,<br>
Aakhir ko dushmani hai asar ko dua ke saath</i>

Henceforth I'll pray for separation from my love,<br>
Prayer and dispensation are deep hostile after all
]

--- Haider Ali 'Aatish'

<i>payaambar</i> : messenger<br>
<i>mayassar</i> : afforded<br>
<i>gair</i> : stranger<br>
<i>sharh</i> : describe<br>
<i>rafookar</i> : one who mends torn clothes<br>
<i>mah</i> : moon<br>
<i>mehr</i> : sun<br>
<i>habeeb</i> : lover<br>
<i>aseer</i> : captives<br>
<i>aalam-e-barghashta-taala'i</i> : caprice of ill fortune<br>
<i>baaraan</i> : rain<br>
