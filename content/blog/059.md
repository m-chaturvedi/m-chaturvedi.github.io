In this post, we discuss an important distinction between Eastern and Western
cultures. We note that by East, we mean how historically the East has been
associated with spirituality and the West with materialism, but we believe that
the contemporary East and West cannot be characterized by their historical
meanings.

As we wrote in a previous post, when perceived reality is understood only
through the mind, it can be characterized as a cognitive bias, and empirical
truth then has to be modelled differently[1][2][3]. This, we believe, is a
fundamental difference between Eastern and Western cultures. The spirituality
in the traditional East stems from the assumption of characterizing perceived
reality as a cognitive bias, or _mithya_ (untruth) as a result of the force of
Maya in Advaita Vedanta and other Eastern religions[4].

Thus, we put forth a hypothesis as to what distinguishes the historical concepts
with which the Eastern and Western cultures are associated.
